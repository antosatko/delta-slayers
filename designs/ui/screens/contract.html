The bell beneath the mill

A missing miller, an old debt, and a bell that rings long after the village has gone to sleep.

018

The matter at hand

The mill's bell has rung at the wrong hour for nine nights. At first, Greybridge blamed a cracked clapper or a drunk watchman. Then the miller stopped answering the door. The guild sent two apprentices to investigate; one returned with the bell's rope tied around his wrist and no memory of the walk home.

The old watercourse runs beneath the west foundation. The villagers call what moves there a , though the word is older than the village and may describe a debt rather than a creature.

What is known

The bell was cast before the bridge had a name. It was rung to mark a — a portion paid to the guild each harvest. Records suggest the miller found a second inscription beneath the lip: a name scratched away, and a date belonging to the .

“Do not ring the bell. Do not follow any voice that answers from below the floorboards. If the miller is alive, bring him back. If he is not, bring the ledger.”Mara Venn, senior clerk

Terms of the contract

Establish what happened at the mill and identify the source of the ringing. Recover the miller's ledger if it can be done without endangering the party. Do not destroy the bell or alter the foundation before the guild has inspected it.

The guild will pay for a complete account. It cannot promise that the truth will be pleasant, nor that every name in the ledger should be read aloud.
